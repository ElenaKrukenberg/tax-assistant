# The measurement has three arms, so the agent cannot take credit for the filter

The agent-versus-questionnaire comparison runs three ways on every profile
fixture, not two: the baseline questionnaire that asks every field, then the
deterministic relevance filter asking the first open question until none remain,
and only then the Interviewer.

The third arm was added after the filter was measured on its own. A form asks all
34 catalogue questions; the filter alone asks 13 of them on a plain profile —
office worker, own car, nothing else — and 20 on a remote worker who bought a
laptop. That saving comes from five cheap yes/no gates, each of which closes a
whole category, and it needs no model at all. A two-arm comparison would have
credited the Interviewer with 34 → 13 and been demolished by the first reader who
asked what the agent contributed.

What is left for the agent is narrower and worth stating in advance: stopping once
the running total can no longer reach the Pauschbetrag, so that further questions
are pointless; ordering the questions that decide most of the amount first, which
matters when somebody abandons the interview halfway; deciding what to do with "I
don't know" rather than only whether to ask; and gap-finding with a reason drawn
from the knowledge base rather than a note that a field is empty.

The arrangement is deliberately able to disprove the project's own thesis. If the
Interviewer shows nothing over the filter on the profiles that were meant to
exercise judgement, the finding to report is that a workflow suffices here — which
is a real result about when an agent is warranted, and a better one than a number
that does not survive being asked about.
